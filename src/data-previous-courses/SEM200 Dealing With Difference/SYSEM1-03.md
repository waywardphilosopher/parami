## Welcome
Welcome back. We're starting this session by reading a session collectively from Augustine's *City of God*.

## Shared reading
We'll read the text out loud from p. 707 (Book XIV, chapter 8), popcorn-style. 

## Discussion
1. What are the sources from which Augustine has learned of the "monstrous races", and how reliable does he think they are?
2. In what ways is this divergence from what he calls "the natural norm" a religious or philosophical problem for him?
3. What analogy does he use to suggest that this diversity, if it exists, might be natural?
4. What is Augustine's definition of "human"?
5. What are the three possibilities Augustine offers for explaining these "monsters"? Are there other possibilities he doesn't include, or that he rules out?

## Monsters: Writing and Thinking (and drawing)
After this, we'll spend 10 minutes writing and thinking about the question:

> Who are the monstrous others (both real and mythical) in your own culture or society?

If you are thinking about **mythical** monsters, you may want to draw as well as write, and add your images of these mythical monsters and short descriptions to the padlet. (Don't do this with **real** monsters).

We'll then share some of your writing and drawings.

## The Monstrosity of Others: Discussion 
Finally, we'll get back into groups to talk about the following questions:

1. What is defined as monstrous within your own culture or society?
2. Is there a connection between our myths and stories about monsters, and the social groups or individuals who we see as monstrous and "other"?
3. What does this idea of the monstrous other say about ourselves?

We'll have a group discussion at the end.  

## Homework
For your homework, I'm going to ask you to read chapter 2 of *The Other* by Kapuściński, 'The Meaning of Difference.'

Think about these three turning points that Kapuściński talks about:

- a) the turning point of anthropologists;
- b) the turning point of Lévinas;
- c) the turning point of multiculturalism.

Do some research to explore any background that may help you understand the text, and to better understand what Kapuściński may be talking about when he discusses these three turning points.
