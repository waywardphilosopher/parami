## On multiculturalism
Kapuściński sees multiculturalism as a reflection of our present global order. There is a potential openness, he says, that is brought about by the end of the Cold War, and the development of better global communications — both electronic and transport links. 

Here, he makes several claims that may have looked more convincing in 2006 when the lectures were first given than in 2023. For example, the claim that there is an end of the era of coups and dictators, and the claim that democracy is becoming fashionable.

But there are also some interesting claims that may be worth exploring. For example:

1. The greater awareness of Others has led to a deepening of questions of identity, as well as a rise in nationalism and racism.
2. Linguistic relativity makes dialogue difficult.
3. There is a tension in our relation to the Other: we both seek them out, and we also mistrust them.
4. Europe is increasingly no longer a reference point for how we think about the world, or how power works within the world.

## Multiculturalism
In the light of this, let's take some time to discuss the following questions

1. What does "multiculturalism" mean to you?
2. Is multiculturalism something we should aspire to? If so, why?
3. What are the down sides of multiculturalism? What threats does it pose?
4. What are the roots of multiculturalism, or what conditions or factors need to be in place for there to be a multicultural society?
5. What conditions or factors might prevent the arising of a multicultural society?
