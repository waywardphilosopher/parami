## Welcome back!
Welcome back! Today, we're going to be starting our first case study in otherness. We're going to be looking at race, and reading cultural theorist Stuart Hall alongside a text about race and ethnicity in Myanmar. This should take us through the next four or five sessions, after which we'll move on to talking about a closely-related issue in Southeast Asia: the politics of indigeneity.

## Thinking about Race and Ethnicity
In today's session, we are going to get started by thining about race and ethnicity in our own contexts.

1. What do race and ethnicity mean to you? Do they mean the same thing to you? If not, what are the differences?
2. What race and/or ethnicity do you identify with/as?
3. In what ways are race and ethnicity significant factors in your own community/communities?
4. How have ideas of race / ethnicity affected you personally?

After you've had five minutes to make notes on these questions, I'll put you in breakout groups. We'll feed back on the breakout groups afterwards

## Reading
Now I want you to read this essay from Frontier magazine, by the brilliant Than Toe Aung, about notions of race and ethnicity in Burma: https://www.frontiermyanmar.net/en/identity-crisis/. 

As you read, I want you to work on annotating the text together, using the "comment" tool on Google Docs. We'll take 20 minutes.

## Discussion
Finally, in discussion groups, I want you to talk about the following:

1. What did you find most persuasive or compelling about this piece?
2. To what extent does it reflect your own experiences?
3. What challenges does it raise for how we think about the relationship between ethnicity, religion and nationhood?
