## Welcome
This is our final session talking about Toni Morrison's book. We're going to be discussing borders, the movement of populations, and globalisation.

## Writing Exercise
Our first task is a writing exercise. I want you to spend ten minutes writing a piece with the title "crossing the border."  This can be a real border, or a metaphorical border. Just respond to the prompt however you like. 

Then I'll get you to bracket and share.

## First Impressions
We'll start by exploring the text in breakout groups. In your groups, I want you to talk about the following:

1. What parts of this final chapter interested you, or drew your attention?
2. What questions do you have about the text?

We'll talk this through in the large group afterwards.


## Borders and Globalisation
There is a complex set of ideas here around borders and globalisation. We'll explore some of these in the coming weeks, when we turn to our case studies. But I want us to focus on a few issues in the text.

1. Morrison says that global migration is a movement of the colonised to the seat of the colonisers. What are the implications of this?
2. Morrison says, "Globalization, hailed with the same vigor as was manifest destiny, internationalism, etc., has reached a level of majesty in our imagination." What does this mean, and do you think she is right?
3. Globalization and borders exist in a complex and dynamic relationship. How do they relate to each other?
