## Welcome back!

This session is going to be delivered asynchronously, as I'll be at a conference in Korea. 

Following on from Tania Bruguera and Immigrant Movement International's migrant manifesto, we'll be looking at Susan Banki's paper "Art is happening in Myanmar, and outside of it: transnational solidarity art" in *Globalizations* 2023. We'll be talking about the following themes.

- Art
- Activism
- Transnationalism
- Diaspora and the movement of human populations
- Solidarity

## Background
The 2021 coup leading to a flowering of solidarity art that had a specific political purpose, that aimed to raise money for those in the CDM, and that sought audiences overseas. 

In her paper Banki quotes Jacqueline Adams as referring to solidarity art as "art made by individuals experiencing state violence and economic hardship, which others distribute, sell, and buy to express solidarity with the artists and give them financial support."

The paper by Banki explores art and activism, or resistance art — the places where art and social justice meet. Banki argues that solidarity art potentially risks reinforcing power relationships that it seeks to dismantle, increasing Global North / South divide. But she goes on to say that that solidarity art happening in Myanmar avoids some of these issues, and may help support transnational human rights activism.

In this session, I want you to do the following. 

## Task 1: Reading Task
Read through the PDF (shared online) catalogue of the FIGHTING FEAR exhibition. It is 58 pages long, but many of the pages are artworks. So it shouldn't take too long. 

## Task 2: Engaging with the Artworks
Think about which artwork or artist you most respond to. Why is this? Choose one single artwork, re-read the section about the artist who produced it. 

Now free-write for 10 minutes in response to this artwork. Come to the next session, ready to share your chosen artwork, and your reflections on this artwork.

## Task 3: Thinking Through the Theory
Our next task is to think through the theoretical context of these artworks.

Banki talks about three ways in which these artworks may avoid some common criticisms of other projects in activist art from the Global South, when these artworks are exhibited in the Global North: multiple transnationalisms, collective self-representation, and resource reciprocity. What do you understand by these three terms? And how successful do you think these artworks are at resolving these problems.

## Task 4: Migration, Diaspora, Art and Manifestos
Think about the work that you have done on manifestos, and the work of Tania Bruguera. These art networks could not exist without a complex set of transnational links — diaspora communities, international travel, social media, transnational networks of artists and scholars.

Re-read the Migrant Manifesto, and make some notes on any points where you think that Bruguera and IM (International)'s manifesto can be brought into dialogue with Banki's paper.
