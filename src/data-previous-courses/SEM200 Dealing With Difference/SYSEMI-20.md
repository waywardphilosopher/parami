## Welcome back!
Today, we're going to be writing our manifestos. You have the notes that you have worked on in the previous session. So today, you're going to have time in the class to write, collectively edit, and perfect your manifestos.

## Writing Your Manifesto
In the last session, I asked you three questions:

- What do you want? 
- What do you believe? 
- What do you know?

To get us started, I'm going to get you to go back to your notes from the last session, and choose **two sentences** to bracket and share.

Remember, this isn't a chance to go off on a tangent and give a mini-lecture on something you've just thought about. Instead, you need to bracket something **in your written text** and read **directly from the page**.

When you have written down some answers to these questions, you are ready to start your manifesto.

- Write boldly and courageously!
- Don't be cautious or over-careful!
- Say what you really think!
- Have fun!

Give yourself 20 minutes to get a first draft.

## Editing
You want your manifesto to be full of life, to be clear and precise. So now the task is to divide your manifesto into at least **TEN** separate, numbered **fragments**. You have 10 minutes for this. Breaking down your manuscript into individual fragments will help you with the editing later. 

After this, I want you **all** to email me your first drafts (you can cut and paste into a Word document, or send a gmail link) to will.buckingham@parami.edu.mm. 


## Group editing
Now I'm going to put you into groups. In your groups, I want you to collectively edit your manifestos. You need to actively engage in this exercise for this class to work.

In your groups, share your text (through share screen), read your manifesto out loud, and go through each fragment. For each fragment,
you have three options.

a) Keep the fragment as it is (it is full of energy and life) and in the place it is.
b) Keep the fragment as it is (it is full of energy and life) but move it (it is in the wrong place)
c) Rewrite the fragment (it could be better)
d) Discard the fragment (it is dull and lifeless, or it doesn't quite belong in the manifesto)

At the end of this editing session, for which you have 40 minutes, I want you to email me your edited final drafts. This **should be substantially different from your first draft**.

## Feedback and Discussion
We're going to spend the last ten or twenty minutes talking through the process of writing your manifestos, and sharing your work.
