## Looking Back
The next thing I want to do is acknowledge how far we have come. We started off by talking about Otherness. Then we explored the work of Kapuściński in several sessions. We talked about monstrousness, globalisation, history, and multiculturalism, as well as introducing ideas from philosophers such as Lévinas and Augustine.

After this, we spent several weeks on Toni Morrison, asking how fiction can address the question of the Other. We tried some fiction writing of our own, and talked about the relationship between storytelling and the idea of the other. 

The first of our big themes was that of race, which we explored through Stuart Hall, and the idea of "floating signifiers." So we touched on the field of semiotics, and the slipperiness of language. We looked at a case study from Myanmar/Burma, exploring racial otherness and the history of mixed marriage in the country since British colonial times.

Our next big theme was indigineity. We looked at how this idea, of growing global significance, plays out in the field of Burmese politics. And we explored the tensions between notions of indigineity and တိုင်းရင်််းသား or taingyinthar.

Our third theme was migration and cross-border links. We dived into this through the manifesto of the Immigrant Movement International (IM International), and wrote some manifestos of our own. Then we looked at cross-border identities and networks in the context of post-coup art in Myanmar/Burma.

Finally, our fourth theme was gender and sexuality, which we're continuing to look at today. We talked about Simone de Beauvoir, the idea of "woman" as itself a floating signifier (these ideas, once they take hold, crop up again and again), and explored the role of women in Myanmar’s Spring Revolution. Our final reading, *Hypo's Choice*, continues to cut across different cultural contexts, to explore the dilemma of a young man in the mid 2010s in Myanmar/Burma: to become a spirit medium, or an LGBTQI+ activist.
