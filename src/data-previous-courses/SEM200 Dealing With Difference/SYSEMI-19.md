## The Shape of a Manifesto

So:
  
  - A manifesto is a piece of writing that sets out (declares or manifests) your beliefs and aims.
  - These beliefs and aims can be political, social, or artistic.
  - Manifestos aim to shake people up and provoke them (good manifestos have an emotional punch!) 
  - A manifesto is a kind of advertising of what matters most to you (or the group you represent). It helps replace false perceptions with true stories!
  - It is about how you want to remake the world.
  - Effective manifestos can change the way that people think and act (for better and worse).

A manifesto can be about anything you like. But most manifestos have the following characteristics:

#### Style: 
When it comes to style, they are presented in a way that is designed to move or affect the reader.

- Manifestos don’t hold back: they are bold
- They are clear and precise
- They are short (no longer than they need to be)
- They aim to engage the reader’s emotions.

#### Content:

- Manifestos diagnose a problem
- They say why the problem exists
- They present a cure for the diagnosis
- They propose how to bring about this cure.

If you can bring these qualities into your writing, you can start to change people's perceptions about who you are and the things that are important to you. 

## Writing your Manifesto
Today, we're going to write a manifesto about some aspect of the topic of otherness that we've been exploring.

It may be about race, or ethnicity, or gender (which we haven't talked about yet, but that's okay), or migration, or diaspora, or indigeneity — or something else entirely.

To get started, I'm going to get you set you three quick writing exercises..

### Exercise 1:
The first question is **‘What do I want?’** In other words, what is your vision for a changed world. How do you want to shake things up? What do you want to transform?

Write for 5 minutes

### Exercise 2:
The second question is **‘What do I believe?’** This is about your convictions, the things that you hold as true, that you think matter. It is about your beliefs relating to this subject, and why you hold them.

Write for 5 minutes

### Exercise 3:
And the third question is **‘What do I know?’** This is about the experience and knowledge that you are drawing on to make your argument. This may be personal experience, or it may be experience gained through work, or through study, or through reading. 

Again, write for five minutes.
