## Presentation Briefing.
Now on to the presentation. Your brief is to design a presentation of six to eight minutes, with a couple of minutes afterwards questions.

This presentation will explore one aspect of the course that we have covered. 

Presentations will be individual presentations. They will be graded according to the following criteria:

- Ability to formulate a topic or research question
- Quality of argument and expression
- Depth of research
- Clarity of communication
- Imagination and creativity

Presentations will be marked individually. But you can collaborate and ask another student to work with you on delivering. How goes this work? Look at the following example:

> Student A has a presentation on Plato, and decides to stage part of the presentation as a dialogue. Student A asks student B to play the role of dialogue partner. However, the marks for this presentation all belong to student A (not student B)

You may ask why anybody would collaborate, if there's no extra credit. The main reason is that it might make the whole process more fun, collective and enjoyable. And, of course, you could always make an agreement to help each other out: so student B can help out student A (even though student A gets all the marks, as it is their presentation) and then student A can help out student B in turn (and student B gets all the marks).

You can take a creative approach to your presentation, and use other materials such as slides, texts, recordings, animations, interactive elements, whole class activities and so on. But at least 60% of the presentation needs to be you presenting material.

Ready? Okay. We're now going to talk about this in groups.
