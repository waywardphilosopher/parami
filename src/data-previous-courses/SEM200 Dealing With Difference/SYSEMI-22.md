## Rereading Banki
What I now want you to do is go back to Susan Banki's paper.  Remember that Banki is both interested in art in the context of post-coup Myanmar for its own sake, and also in how this provides a model for how artworks may avoid some common criticisms of other projects in activist art from the Global South, when these artworks are exhibited in the Global North. Banki argues that through multiple transnationalisms, collective self-representation, and resource reciprocity, these artworks do not reproduce the power imbalances of the Global South / Global North.

But there are many other interesting things in the paper as well. So we'll take 15 or 20 minutes to re-read the paper in-class. Re-reading is often overlooked. As you read, I want you to do two things:

1. Post at least two questions either about the paper, or provoked by the paper. Post these questions in Zoom, using the Q&A tool (not the chat tool)
2. Bracket at least one section that speaks to you, ready to share.

When we've finished reading, we'll bracket and share. Then I'll get you to upvote the questions you think are most interesting or compelling or important.
