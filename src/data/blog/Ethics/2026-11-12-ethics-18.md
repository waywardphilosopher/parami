## An Envelope

Imagine that you find an envelope with $100 and no way to trace the owner. Write down what you would do and why. 

Now think about the following questions:

- Would this change if someone were watching? 
- Woudl this change if you believed a god was watching? 
- Would it change if you were being recorded.If a camera were recording?

### Prosociality, Religion and Ethis

There are several main claims in this paper, Norenzayan, A., & Shariff, A. F. (2008). The Origin and Evolution of Religious Prosociality. *Science*, *322*(5898), 58–62. [https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1158757](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1158757)

- Surveys show religious people report giving more
- Self-reports can be unreliable (maybe religious people feel they *should* claim to be more generous)
- In behavioral studies, the effect appears mainly when people feel watched or reputation is at stake.
- Reminding people of God, or of secular authority like courts, increases generosity.
- Moralizing gods are more common in larger societies.
- Religious prosociality is mostly directed at the ingroup.

## Thinking Through Motivations for Moral Action

- Donating openly at a public event

## Aside: Is all ethics transactional?

If there's something in this, then maybe our prosociality (our generosity, or goodness, or kindness) is not entirely disinterested. Maybe we do these things because a) we may glean some kind of unexpected benefit and b) we fear social disapproval. For example, generosity often has *some kind of payback* in terms of reputation,  This leads some philosophers, like Derrida, to ask whether there is a gift that escapes "economic" or transactional reasoning (see his book *Counterfeit Money*).
